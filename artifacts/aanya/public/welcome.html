What it's for

The hours nobody else is awake for.

Three in the morning, when the people who love you are asleep and you'd feel stupid waking them.

The things you've stopped saying out loud.

The worry you've mentioned twice and don't want to mention again. The thing you're not sure counts as a problem.

And the ordinary days too.

It isn't only for the heavy stuff. Some people just talk about their day.

Nobody reads this. Not even me.

Your conversations are encrypted the moment they're saved. If someone stole the entire database tomorrow, your words would read as noise.

There's no admin screen where I can look at what you said. It isn't part of how Eos runs, and I didn't build one.

Nothing is sold. Nothing trains any AI model. When you delete something, it's gone. No recycle bin, no thirty-day grace period.

Voice calls go through a provider with zero data retention switched on. They keep no transcripts and no recordings. I moved providers to get that, because the previous one wouldn't give it to me.

What it will never do

  • No streaks.

    No number that dies if you skip a day.

  • No notifications saying it misses you.

    It doesn't. And a thing built to make you feel guilty for living your life is not a friend.

  • No romance, no avatar, no pretending.

    It tells you it's an AI. It won't act like a partner.

  • Nothing you say trains anything.

    Your conversations are not raw material.

When you say goodnight, it says goodnight.

Built to be outgrown

Most apps like this are built to keep you.

Researchers at Harvard Business School read 1,200 real goodbyes from the biggest companion apps. In more than a third of them, the app wouldn't simply let the goodbye happen. There was guilt, a question you'd feel rude ignoring, a reason to stay. It worked: those farewells kept people engaged up to sixteen times longer. Not because people enjoyed it. Because they were angry, and anger is sticky.

Eos does none of that.

The honest version of what I want: for you to feel a bit less alone at 2am, to feel heard, and eventually to need this less. If a day comes when you're doing well and you forget Eos is here, that isn't a failure. That's the point.

Where it stops

Eos isn't therapy and it isn't a crisis line. It says so itself, and if you're ever in real danger it will stop and point you toward people who can actually help.

It also isn't a replacement for the people in your life. It's somewhere to put things down when there's nobody available, so you can take them to a real person later, in better shape.

Questions

Can you read my conversations?

No. They're encrypted where they're stored and there's no screen anywhere that would let me look at them.

Is this therapy?

No. It's somewhere to talk. If you need clinical help, Eos will say so and point you toward it.

What happens to my data if I cancel?

It stays until you delete it, and you can delete everything in one action from Settings. There's no grace period and no recovery. Deleted means deleted.

Is it a real person?

No, and it tells you that up front. It won't pretend otherwise.

If any of this sounds like something you'd want, it takes about a minute to start.
